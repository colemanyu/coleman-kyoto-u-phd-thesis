\section{Conclusion and Future Work} \label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we propose a novel distance measure framework, Piecewise Scaling Distance (PSD), which relaxes the strict assumption of a single uniform scaling factor across the entire time series. 
We presented an exact dynamic programming (DP) algorithm to solve this problem.
To enhance efficiency and prevent pathological segment alignments, we introduced a constraint version, which limits the search space of segment lengths based on given scaling factors. 
To enhance computational efficiency, we integrated two optimization techniques for the general PSD framework.
In addition, we propose incorporating a lower-bounding strategy to accelerate PSDTW.
Our experimental results demonstrate the necessity and effectiveness of PSD for identifying matches between a query $Q$ under piecewise scaling distortions and a set of candidate time series.

We summarize the key deliverables.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Optimal phase cut: The algorithm outputs the cutting points $(i_P, j_P)$ (i.e., the ending points of the $p$\textsuperscript{th} segment) that partition the two input series into $P$ contiguous aligned segments.
  Hence, it reveals the regime in the time series.
  \item Guided distance: The cutting points mentioned above can guide the application of other distance measures to compute the overall distance between the two series.
  \item Phase-specific scaling factors: It identifies the local scaling factors for each phase, defined by the lengths between $i_p - i_{p-1}$ and $j_p - j_{p-1}$. 
  Hence, it reveals how expression rates vary across segments of the two series.
  \item It provides the final distance measure that is robust to piecewise scaling. Hence, it can be used to achieve better accuracy for higher-level tasks that use it as a subroutine.
\end{itemize}

We discuss the potential impact and implications.
\begin{itemize}
    \item It improves accuracy when piecewise scaling distortion is present in the data, which is common in applications such as ``Query-by-Humming'' or ``Gesture Recognition'', where humans vary the speeds in different phases.
    \item The framework mitigates the need for manual segmentation by automatically identifying the optimal cutting points and reviewing optimal segment structures. It facilitates the continuous monitoring of sequential action without requiring a metronome or post-manual partitioning of the raw data stream.
\end{itemize}

We discuss the challenges and future work here.
\begin{itemize}
    \item The number of pieces $P$ currently require manual definition. 
    Although we can find the best value by testing a range of values, designing a more efficient method is a challenge.
    \item The computational time of PSD is slow. Hence, future work should focus on improving its efficiency.
    A lower bound specifically designed for PSD can admissibly prune unpromising candidates.
    We note that many subsequences that share the same partition (the suffix) are involved in the computation of the internal similarity. It would be beneficial to reuse these computations to speed up the computation. 
    It may be possible to reuse the result of the computation on the shorter segment to compute the result for the longer one.
    A similar idea can be found in studies accelerating DTW.
    Incremental DTW~\cite{leodolter2021incdtw} allows for the reuse of the accumulating cost matrix for an incremental calculation of DTW for updating interim results.

    A difficulty in applying a similar idea to PSD is that PSD computes the distance measure between two growing segments after segment scaling. 
    The interpolation alters the subsequence structure.
    It prevents the straightforward reuse of the computed result from the shorter segment in the longer segment.
\end{itemize}
